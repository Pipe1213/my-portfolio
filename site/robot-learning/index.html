Selected work, 2026

Robot learning from few demonstrations

Question
At each number of demonstrations, how does a fine-tuned pretrained vision-language-action model compare with a policy trained from scratch?
Setup
LeRobot 0.6.2, LIBERO-Object in simulation (Franka arm), 3 pick-and-place tasks, 5, 10, 20 or all (45–50) demonstrations per task
Models
ACT, 52M parameters, trained without robot pretraining (ImageNet image encoder). SmolVLA, 450M parameters, fine-tuned from lerobot/smolvla_base (action expert only, about 100M trained)
Scale and cost
60 trained models, 180 evaluated checkpoints, 9,000 simulated episodes; $31.84 in cloud compute

Code on GitHub60 models on Hugging Face

Result

SmolVLA fine-tuned on 5 demonstrations per task succeeded 78% of the time. ACT trained on all of them, about 47 per task, reached 55%. Every SmolVLA training seed with 5 demonstrations beat every ACT seed trained on all of them. The gap shrank from 60 to 32 points as data was added, but it never closed. SmolVLA is also the larger model and cost more to train, so this is about data efficiency, not compute.

Stability

ACT’s results depended a lot on training randomness. If a run’s three evaluated checkpoints were equally good, sampling 50 episodes would still give them a standard deviation of 5 to 6 points. ACT’s checkpoints had 17.8; SmolVLA’s had 5.3, what sampling alone predicts. A paired test (Cochran’s Q, on the same 50 scenes) found significant differences between checkpoints in 30 of 36 ACT runs and in 2 of 24 SmolVLA runs. One ACT run scored 26, 5 and 6 out of 50 at its three checkpoints.

At 10 demonstrations, ACT’s three seeds went from 17% to 54%, a spread as large as its whole gain from 5 demonstrations to all of them.

How the policies fail

I coded 90 evaluation videos without knowing which method produced them. Almost every failure of both methods started with a missed or dropped grasp. ACT usually tried again after a miss but recovered only once; its typical failure was hovering over the object until the time limit. SmolVLA’s failures were mostly a single miss with no second attempt. Both clips were picked at random from the coded videos of each kind.

Limits

  • Three tasks from one simulated benchmark, one robot, no real hardware.
  • Two different models, not an ablation: SmolVLA and ACT differ in pretraining, but also in size, architecture and language input.
  • Compute is not matched; the question is data efficiency, not training cost.
  • ACT used LeRobot’s default settings plus re-planning every 10 actions; a tuned ACT might do better.
  • The second training seed was added after seeing the first seed’s results, and applied to every run.
  • The three tasks were chosen among LIBERO-Object tasks the published SmolVLA LIBERO checkpoint already solved.
  • Evaluation settings were chosen on the test scenes of one task (BBQ sauce).

Next question

How much of the gap comes from SmolVLA’s robot pretraining itself? The next step is to fine-tune SmolVLA twice with everything else equal: once from its pretrained action expert and once from a freshly initialised one. After that, training on different subsets of the same size would show whether the dips come from specific demonstrations.
